# Research design

## The question

The project began by asking whether the contractual protections of Indian corporate bonds, their later monitoring disclosures and any reported breaches can be made traceable at the level of the individual instrument.

Investigating the sources changed the question. Trustees are already required to publish breach reports per ISIN, and contract documents often exist online, so the gap is not secrecy. The sharper question is:

> **For listed Indian non-convertible debentures, how much of the path from a contractual protection to a reported breach of it can an independent researcher reconstruct from public sources, and exactly where does that path break?**

Each part can be measured from a defined search process, and none of it depends on knowing what happened inside any company.

## Why this framing

SEBI's Security and Covenant Monitoring System already holds covenants, trust deeds and monitoring data, for registered issuers, trustees and rating agencies. An independent project should not try to recreate that system. What it can add is an audit of the public record: what an outsider can see, how consistently, and with what evidence. So the unit of analysis is a statement in a document rather than an inferred fact about a bond.

## Population and sample

**Population of interest.** Listed, rupee-denominated non-convertible debentures of Indian issuers.

**Sample in this release.** ISINs listed in the covenant-breach reports (Annex-VIIA, Table 5) published by six debenture trustees for the quarter ended 30 June 2026, together with:

- both published versions of Axis Trustee's report for that quarter, and its later report as of 29 October 2026;
- two further Aye Finance ISINs, kept to separate "reported in another quarter" and "trustee not established" from a reported breach;
- one Satya ISIN, INE982X08042, which the issuer's auditor reports as non-compliant and the trustee's report does not list.

For 11 issuers in the sample, the issuer's own statement of covenant compliance for the same quarter was also sought, as an independent channel. See [research-agenda.md](research-agenda.md).

**What this selection means.** The sample is chosen on the outcome. It is suitable for asking how traceable a reported breach is, and unsuitable for estimating how often breaches occur, or for comparing trustees' or issuers' behaviour. Counts by trustee partly reflect how many issues each one administers, which this release does not measure.

**Trustees.** SEBI's register listed 26 debenture trustees on 2 November 2026, many of them banks. The register's second page could not be retrieved. This release covers Axis Trustee, Beacon, Catalyst, IDBI Trusteeship, MITCON Credentia and Vistra ITCL, with no report located for SBICAP Trustee and Orbis. Others were not reviewed.

## Hypotheses and what the evidence says

| Hypothesis from the original brief | What this release shows | Standing |
|---|---|---|
| Contractual protections can be reconstructed systematically from original public documents | Clause wording often survives in trustees' notices and reports, but no original contract could be retrieved through the pipeline, and 29 of 38 searches found none | Partly, and not from original documents. Open until contracts are ingested |
| Individual covenants can be linked to later disclosures | A trustee cites a dated deed or trustee agreement for 52 of 93 listed ISINs. 14 ISINs were linked to a transcribed clause, 13 of them consistent. Issuers' own statements name the breached covenant for 17 | Feasible where a trustee or the issuer names the clause. Fails where only a description survives |
| Public observability can be measured | Status per ISIN and period, a search log, and a trace from report to contract are all computed | Supported, with the sample's limits |
| The same provision or event is represented consistently | Within one trustee, levels agree in 24 of 24 comparisons and dates in 37 of 41. Between issuer and trustee, 17 of 22 comparisons agree; required levels, deed histories and one compliance conclusion differ | Mixed: a party agrees with itself; different parties disagree in ways one source cannot show |
| Records can support financial document AI | Anchors and statuses offer real tasks, but scanned and outlined files and short quotations need care, and two sources reserve AI training | Promising, not yet attempted |

## Measurements

All are defined in code and regenerated by `brdo report`.

- **Status per ISIN and period** (`status.py`): derived mechanically, with a separate `basis` for each kind of absence.
- **Trace to the contract** (`linkage_funnel`): how many listed ISINs reach each stage.
- **Specificity**: how much of the contract a report prints for each ISIN and cell (clause text, required and actual levels, clause number only, description only).
- **Report versus notice** (`consistency.py`): dates and stated levels compared within one trustee's own publications.
- **Revisions** (`consistency.py`): what changed between a report and the version that supersedes it.
- **Date usage**: for each report, how often the "actual breach" date equals the quarter-end or the detection date.

## Results in this release

These are descriptive and limited to the sample. The figures come from the [observability report](observability-report.md).

**Where the path breaks.** Of 93 listed ISINs, 59 are named in a notice to holders, 52 have a deed date stated by the trustee (for seven of them a debenture trustee agreement rather than a deed), and 44 have the clause or required level printed in the report itself. For 17, the issuer's or its auditor's own statement names the breached covenant. None has its contract retrieved. The chain usually breaks at the last step, and for a reason of access rather than existence.

**Access decides the outcome more than existence does.** Contract documents were located for a handful of ISINs on exchange or platform pages that bar automated retrieval. Search engines surface these files while the exchange refuses identified programs, so the record is public in a way that does not extend to reproducible research.

**Trustee practice varies widely.** Catalyst prints clause text for 53 of 58 ISIN-level cells. IDBI prints a description for 40 of 43, though it alone prints required and actual levels side by side in two cases. Catalyst also publishes the follow-up letters that show meeting adjournments.

**Dates do not mean one thing.** Catalyst's note says the date of actual breach is the quarter for which a certificate was submitted, and 8 of its 14 serial numbers print the quarter-end while 6 print the detection date. IDBI's note defines the date as that of the auditor's certificate or the due date. A "detection lag" computed across reports would therefore be meaningless, so the report presents intervals only per report and warns against comparison.

**Reported follow-up.** All 14 of Catalyst's serials read "Awaiting instructions from Debenture Holders". IDBI's report more often states what happened next, including cures and meetings where no holder attended. A later letter can change the picture, as with the Axis waiver, so a snapshot of any one report understates what is known.

**Independent corroboration.** Issuers' own statements for the June 2026 quarter could be retrieved for three issuers. Set beside the trustees' reports they gave 22 ISIN-level comparisons: 17 agree that a breach occurred, 3 differ and 2 cannot be compared. Infinity's auditor reported compliance with all covenants for two ISINs that the trustee lists for a governance breach. Satya's auditor reported a breach for an ISIN that the trustee's report omits. Infinity's management statement covers financial covenants only, so it cannot answer a governance listing. The agreements are often exact: sub-clause lists, required levels and, in one case, the measured ratio.

**Contract versions.** An issuer's intimation under regulation 59, with the exchange's in-principle approval annexed, sets out the existing and proposed wording of one covenant. It accounts for the issuer's December 2025 figure and the trustee's notice. The trustee's quarterly report and the issuer's June 2026 figure still do not fit. The executed amendment deed has not been retrieved.

**Absence means little by itself.** INE501X07570 has a trustee notice of a breach for the March 2026 quarter and is not listed in the June report. The dataset records `reported_breach` for one and `no_report_found` for the other, and says nothing about whether anything changed.

## Threats to validity

- **Selection on the outcome**, as above.
- **Search dependence.** Contract searches used a general web search tool whose index and results change. A `not_found` means not found through the logged query on that date. Fingerprint searches with quoted covenant wording did not do better than ISIN searches.
- **Issuer statements are self-reports.** Auditors give them limited assurance in negative form. A statement's stated scope decides what it can answer, and the comparison reports a scope mismatch as not comparable rather than as agreement.
- **Search engines miss issuer filings.** Two of the three issuer statements were found through a search engine, and one only by opening the issuer's regulation 62 page. Not finding a statement this way says little about whether it was published.
- **Single-pass curation.** There has been no independent second review, so classification errors are possible. Category, specificity and action fields are annotations and may be challenged.
- **Printed errors.** Sources contain typos and possible mistakes, which are kept verbatim where quoted. ISINs were checked with their check digit, but a valid ISIN can still be the wrong one, as `dvara-isin-in-notice` suggests.
- **Issuer identity.** Issuer records rest partly on single values seen on depository pages.
- **Numeric comparison is narrow.** Agreement of levels does not show that two clauses mean the same thing.
- **Versions are point-in-time.** A later upload may replace a file, which is why each document is identified by its hash.

## What would change these conclusions

- Automated access granted by an exchange, or a reliable public index of offer documents, would move the point where the trace breaks.
- Ingesting contracts could show that reports misquote clauses more often than these comparisons can detect.
- A comparison sample of instruments with no reported breach could show whether the notices seen here are typical of a trustee's publications generally.
